Bonzah × Facio
The perfect demo for Brandon Rockow
A source-grounded build, rehearsal and acceptance specification for the 10 September 2026 demonstration.
This document converts the introduction meeting, follow-up emails, supplied July production workbook, sample certificates, public Bonzah flows and the current Facio Gen2 implementation into one controlled demonstration plan.
01  Executive direction
What the demonstration must prove—and what it must deliberately avoid claiming.
The outcome
Brandon should leave believing that Facio can preserve the operational reliability he values while removing the rigidity he dislikes. The proof is one coherent transaction, not a tour of disconnected features.
Success signals
01  Continuity. The same quote ID, customer, risk and premium logic survive across the API, back office, documents, endorsement and claims journey.
02  Control. Rules, rates, documents and required fields are versioned, effective-dated and auditable—not hard-coded into a one-off portal.
03  Distribution fit. The story speaks first to DTC, portal and embedded/API. Co-branded pages and OTA distribution remain credible extensions, not the centre of the demo.
04  Truthfulness. Every capability carries a visible status label: LIVE GEN2, BONZAH DEMO BUILD or CONCEPT. No audience member should confuse a mock settlement view with a live payment rail.
05  Commercial relevance. The demonstration ties flexibility to attachment rate, partner growth, servicing cost, receivables risk and speed of launching changes.

07  Demo surface specifications
Every screen must answer a Brandon question; anything else is visual noise.
Interaction standards
■  The browser title, logo, colour and product language say Bonzah; “Abbeygate” never appears in the customer-facing flow.
■  All decisions include a customer-safe explanation and an internal rule reference available in the back office.
■  Every total has a coverage-level breakdown. Never show an unexplained final premium.

08  Payment, settlement and document issuance
Separate what the platform can prove today from the partner money-movement design Brandon wants.
Payment state machine
Current Gen2 payment evidence is CardCorp/OPPWA-oriented. The Bonzah demo should therefore use a gateway-neutral interface with a simulated processor, while the implementation plan treats Stripe and ACH/autodraw as separately scoped adapters.
Partner receivable concept
Certificate pack
For each purchased coverage, generate a separate customer-facing certificate whose dynamic fields—insured, named drivers, policy number, effective/expiry dates, limits, deductible and vehicle/rental details—are sourced from the bound transaction. Fixed producer/carrier/wording blocks come from a versioned, customer-approved template.
09  Endorsement and FNOL specifications
The date-change endorsement is the central operational proof because it represents 94.6% of supplied July endorsement rows.
Date-change acceptance flow
01  Start from the bound policy. Operator selects “Change rental dates” and sees the current effective/expiry dates and policy transaction number.
02  Enter the new return date. The demo extends the risk by two days before the original expiry; the maximum-period rule is re-evaluated.
03  Re-rate canonically. The same rating spine calculates the incremental coverage premium and returns before/after values plus a pricing integrity stamp.
04  Confirm money. Direct payment or partner receivable records the delta; issuance remains gated until the required state is verified.
05  Issue a new transaction. A versioned endorsement snapshot, reason code, actor and timestamps are created. The original bound snapshot remains unchanged.
06  Reissue and notify. Affected certificates are regenerated, delivery is recorded and reporting receives a distinct endorsement transaction.
FNOL flow
The demonstration should reflect the current Bonzah claim form rather than invent a generic two-field notice. It must support a short-lived prefilled link, save/resume and an evidence checklist.

Architecture guardrails
■  Keep the Bonzah product package and tenant configuration separate from shared policy, transaction, payment, reporting and MCP modules.

13  Acceptance criteria
The demo is ready only when the following behaviours pass on a clean synthetic environment.
Stop-ship defects
■  Any real customer, partner, policy, payment or contact data is visible.
■  The DTC and API prices disagree for the same canonical risk without an explained channel term.
■  A policy issues before verified payment/receivable state or after an altered client-side total.
■  A certificate contains a field that does not match the transaction shown onscreen.
■  The endorsement overwrites the original policy snapshot or fails to create new documents.
■  The presenter must describe a mocked settlement or compatibility path as live.
