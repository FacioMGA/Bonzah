Inward Business Model – PAS Configuration Requirements
1. Business model / placement structure
The MGA will be receiving inward business through follow subscription placements.
The anticipated structure is that Swiss Re or Munich Re will provide the lead line, typically 50% of the risk, with Ark participating as a following insurer for approximately 5%–15% per risk.
The intention is that Ark will participate on a follow basis behind the lead insurer rather than acting as lead on these placements.
As the business develops, we expect additional Lloyd’s syndicates to join the panel. This is because Ark is unlikely to be able to provide the full 50% follow capacity required on every risk. The PAS therefore needs to support multiple following capacity providers on a single policy/risk, with each provider having its own percentage line.
The precise panel composition may therefore vary from risk to risk.

The PAS therefore needs to be able to record the signed/participating line for each capacity provider, rather than simply storing a single insurer for the policy.
It should also be possible to distinguish between:

  *   the lead insurer / lead line;
  *   each individual following insurer / syndicate;
  *   the percentage line written by each participant; and
  *   the resulting premium and other financial amounts attributable to each participant.

3. MGA commission
VUW.ai will receive a 7.5% commission in relation to the business.
VUW.ai does not hold client money permissions and therefore will not receive or hold the underlying client premium.
The anticipated cash/settlement flow is:
Client / Broker → Guy Carpenter (GC) → Bureau → Ark / relevant capacity providers
The commission will be paid to VUW.ai through a commission drop-down arrangement, expected to be made by either Guy Carpenter or the bureau. The precise mechanism is still to be confirmed.
From a PAS perspective, we therefore need to be able to:

  1.  calculate/record the 7.5% MGA commission;
  2.  identify the commission recipient as VUW.ai;
  3.  distinguish the commission from the underlying premium due to capacity providers;
  4.  record the relevant settlement party; and
  5.  support reconciliation where the commission is paid separately from the underlying premium.

The PAS should not assume that VUW.ai is receiving the client’s premium directly.
4. Premium and settlement
The PAS needs to support the fact that the MGA is arranging/administering the business but is not the holder of client money.
The system should therefore be capable of recording the different financial interests in the transaction, including:

  *   gross written premium;
  *   premium attributable to each capacity provider based on their line;
  *   MGA commission;
  *   brokerage/other deductions where applicable;
  *   taxes/charges where applicable;
  *   return premium where applicable; and
  *   the resulting net settlement amounts.

The exact settlement/bureau arrangements may vary depending on the final operating model, so we would prefer the PAS configuration to retain flexibility around the settlement party.
5. Bordereaux
During the initial implementation phase, the business will use the Bordereaux 5.2 template for reporting/settlement purposes.
The expectation is that the PAS will initially generate/populate the required information in accordance with this template, with the longer-term objective being to automate the bordereaux process once the platform is fully configured.
Accordingly, the implementation should ensure that the underlying policy and transaction data captured in the PAS is sufficient to produce the required Bordereaux 5.2 output.
6. Mid-term adjustments (MTAs)
The current QuoteBox configuration does not support MTAs, and this functionality is not expected to be available for approximately another six months.
Consequently, during the interim period, MTA endorsements will be processed manually.
The PAS therefore needs to accommodate manually processed MTA transactions, including the ability to record the resulting changes to:

  *   policy/risk information;
  *   premium;
  *   commission;
  *   capacity-provider shares;
  *   effective date of the adjustment; and
  *   any associated accounting/settlement information.

The MTA should ultimately be reflected against the underlying policy and should not require the creation of an entirely separate unrelated policy record.
Once QuoteBox MTA functionality becomes available, we expect this process to move towards automation.
7. Cancellations, return premiums and other policy transactions
The same principles should apply to other policy transactions, including cancellations, return premiums, reinstatements and other endorsements.
Where a transaction changes the premium, the PAS should be able to calculate the corresponding impact on each capacity provider’s share and on the 7.5% MGA commission, where applicable.
For example, if a policy has:

  *   50% Swiss Re lead;
  *   10% Ark follow; and
  *   40% other following capacity,

then an MTA generating an additional £10,000 of gross premium should allow the PAS to determine the corresponding premium allocation to each participant based on their written line.
8. Key PAS requirement
The key requirement is that the PAS treats the risk/policy as a single placement with multiple capacity participants, while retaining the individual line and financial information for each participant.
We therefore need the system to support:

  *   one policy/risk;
  *   one overall placement;
  *   one lead capacity provider;
  *   multiple follow capacity providers;
  *   individual percentage lines for each provider;
  *   premium allocation by provider;
  *   commission calculation;
  *   transaction-level adjustments;
  *   manual processing during the interim MTA period; and
  *   reporting/settlement through the Bordereaux 5.2 process.

The panel is expected to evolve over time, so the configuration should not be dependent on Ark being the only following capacity provider.

5. How the PAS should represent the policy
We would envisage the PAS holding a single policy record with, at minimum, the following relationships:

  *   Insured
  *   Broker / producing broker
  *   Inception date
  *   Expiry date
  *   Gross premium
  *   Currency
  *   Overall placement information

Capacity / subscription

  *   Lead capacity provider
  *   Following capacity providers
  *   Written line for each participant
  *   Premium attributable to each participant
  *   Relevant commission/deduction information
  *   Settlement party

For the example above, the PAS would therefore have one policy but four capacity records:

  1.  Swiss Re – 50%
  2.  Ark – 10%
  3.  Lloyd’s Syndicate A – 15%
  4.  Lloyd’s Syndicate B – 25%

This structure is important because the panel may change between risks and we expect additional Lloyd’s syndicates to join the panel over time.

8. Bordereaux
Until the platform is fully automated, we expect the relevant policy and transaction information to be reported using the Bordereaux 5.2 template.
The PAS should therefore retain sufficient transaction-level data to support the production of this bordereaux.
The long-term objective is for the bordereaux to be generated automatically from the PAS rather than requiring manual manipulation of the underlying policy data.

9. Summary of the PAS behaviour we are looking for
In simple terms, the PAS needs to support the following model:
One risk → one policy → multiple capacity providers → individual written lines → premium allocation → commission calculation → transaction-level adjustments → bordereaux / settlement reporting.
The capacity panel should be configurable at policy/risk level because the following capacity may differ from one risk to another.
Ark is expected to participate at approximately 5%–15% per risk, with the lead insurer providing approximately 50%, initially Swiss Re or Munich Re. Other Lloyd’s syndicates are expected to participate as additional followers as the panel develops.
